# Research on Radar Interference Methods: Shadowing Techniques

The flash interference in operational use is a task-level cooperative noise interference, that is, two or more jammers located within the radar antenna beams interfere with the radar at certain frequencies alternately, and each jammer's working bandwidth must be able to cover the radar signal bandwidth. Flash interference causes the radar antenna to oscillate back and forth between multiple sources of interference, thereby disrupting the normal operation of the radar servo tracking system, making it an effective way to counter single pulse radar. This section will introduce the basic principles of flash interference methods, and analyze the impact of various interference parameters on their effectiveness and the principles for parameter selection through an example of two-source flash interference.
From the perspective of working sequence, flash interference can be divided into synchronous interference and asynchronous interference. From the perspective of signal phase relationship, flash interference can be divided into coherent interference and non-coherent interference. Among them, non-coherent synchronous flash interference is the most widely used, and this paper mainly studies it.
The schematic diagram of synchronous flash interference shows that both jammers transmit square modulated pulse signals with a 50% duty cycle, maintaining synchronization in time domain.
For two targets located within the radar angle resolution unit, the radar will track to the energy centroid of the signal, and shift towards the target with greater energy. It is precisely by utilizing this principle that flash interference allows the radar angle tracking system to oscillate back and forth between the two jammers, unable to stabilize tracking of the target. Once the jammers are turned off, the radar will lose the target, thus reducing the hit rate of the weapon system.

The interference signal styles mainly include deceptive interference signals and suppressive interference signals. For flash interference, when using forward-looking distance or speed deception interference, the differences in distance and speed can easily distinguish between two interferers, and it is difficult to achieve a large signal-to-noise ratio with deceptive interference signals, making it challenging for radar antennas to pivot back and forth between the two interferers; when using noise interference signals (radio frequency noise interference, noise modulation interference, noise frequency modulation interference, and noise phase modulation interference), not only can a larger signal-to-noise ratio be formed, but it can also suppress radar signals, increasing the measurement error of radar measurements, making it difficult for radar to distinguish between targets based on distance and speed. Therefore, when implementing flash interference, noise interference signals should be chosen.

Flash frequency Generally speaking, the response time of radar angle tracking systems is about 0.1s~2.0s, and the flash frequency should be at the same order of magnitude. If the flash frequency is too high, the radar angle tracking system will not respond in time due to inertia; if the flash frequency is too low, the interferer may be tracked by the radar for a long time, thereby being precisely hit.
Furthermore, when selecting the flash frequency, attention should also be paid to ensure that half of the flash period is less than the radar angle tracking system's response time, so as to guarantee that when switching between interferers, the radar antenna has not yet stabilized tracking of the interferer, and there will still be a significant angle error output.
Interference Setup: The selection of the distance between interference machines significantly affects the effectiveness of flash interference. Whether it's missile or aircraft synchronization, as the interference platform approaches the anti-aircraft radar dynamically, the angle formed by the flash source and the radar gradually increases. When this angle exceeds the radar's resolution angle, the flash source is resolved, rendering the flash interference ineffective. At this point, the distance between the interference machines can be optimized for deployment, with a reasonable distance effectively reducing the weapon system's hit rate.
